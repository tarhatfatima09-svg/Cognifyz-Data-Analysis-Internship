# Level 1 – Task 3: Price Range Distribution

## Objective
To analyze the distribution of restaurants across different price range categories and calculate the percentage of restaurants in each category.

## Methodology
1. Loaded the restaurant dataset using Python and Pandas.
2. Checked for missing values in the `Price range` column.
3. Counted the number of restaurants in each price range category.
4. Calculated the percentage of restaurants in each category using the total of 9,551 restaurants.
5. Created a bar chart using Matplotlib to visualize the distribution.
6. Exported the results to a CSV file and saved the chart as a PNG image.

## Results

| Price Range | Restaurant Count | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 4,444 | 46.53% |
| 2 | 3,113 | 32.59% |
| 3 | 1,408 | 14.74% |
| 4 | 586 | 6.14% |
| **Total** | **9,551** | **100.00%** |

Missing values in the Price range column: 0.

## Key Findings
- Price Range 1 contains the largest number of restaurants, accounting for 46.53% of the dataset.
- Price Range 2 is the second-largest category, representing 32.59% of restaurants.
- Together, Price Ranges 1 and 2 account for 79.12% of all restaurants.
- Price Range 4 has the fewest restaurants, with only 6.14% of the total.
- The distribution indicates that restaurants in the lower price range categories dominate this dataset.

## Conclusion
The analysis shows that most restaurants belong to Price Ranges 1 and 2, while relatively few belong to Price Range 4. The bar chart clearly illustrates the distribution across the four categories.

## Tools Used
Python, Pandas, Matplotlib, and Visual Studio Code.
